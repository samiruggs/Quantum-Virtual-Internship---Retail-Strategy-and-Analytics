# Quantum-Virtual-Internship---Retail-Strategy-and-Analytics

## Retail Strategy & Analytics – Task 1

## Project Overview
This project analyses retail transaction and customer data to understand purchasing behaviour within the **chips category**. The objective is to identify key drivers of sales performance—including customer segmentation, product preferences, and temporal trends—and translate these insights into actionable retail strategy recommendations.

---

## Project Questions
- Which customer segments contribute most to chip sales?
- How do **affluence** and **lifestage** influence purchasing behaviour?
- Which **brands** and **pack sizes** are most popular?
- Are there identifiable **seasonal or daily sales patterns**?
- Are there statistically significant differences in purchasing behaviour across customer groups?

---

## Data Sources
- **Transaction data**: Item-level retail transactions (date, store, product, quantity, sales value)
- **Customer data**: Demographic attributes including lifestage and affluence
- **Product data**: Brand names and pack sizes for chip products

_Data provided as part of the Quantium Virtual Internship Program._

---

## Tools Used
- **SQL**
- **Data visualisation** (time series and categorical plots)

---

## Data Cleaning & Preparation
Key steps included:
- Filtering the dataset to include **chips products only**
- Converting pack sizes to numeric values
- Standardising brand names
- Removing non-relevant product categories
- Aggregating data by customer, store, and time period
- Handling missing values and checking data consistency

---

## Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA)
EDA focused on:
- Sales distribution across stores
- Brand and pack-size preferences
- Monthly and daily sales trends
- Customer purchasing frequency
- Differences across affluence and lifestage segments

Sales were generally stable across time, with slight peaks in **March** and **December**, and marginally higher activity during **weekends**.

---

## Results & Findings

- Mainstream customers drive the highest purchase volume, followed by Budget customers

- Older Singles/Couples, Retirees, and Older Families are the most frequent purchasers

- Kettle dominates brand sales

- The 175g pack size is the clear customer preference

- Sales peak slightly in March and December, likely due to Easter and Christmas

- Weekend sales are marginally higher than weekday sales

- The top 20 customers display highly consistent purchasing behaviour

---

## Recommendations

- Prioritise Mainstream and Budget segments in pricing and promotions

- Maintain strong availability of top brands and 175g pack sizes

- Target Older Singles/Couples, Retirees, and Older Families with tailored offers

- Align promotions with seasonal peaks and weekends

- Exercise caution with price increases due to the category’s price sensitivity

---

## Limitations

- Analysis is based on historical data and does not capture competitor actions or marketing campaigns
- Customer behaviour may change over time and should be periodically reassessed
